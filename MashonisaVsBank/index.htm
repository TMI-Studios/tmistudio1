What is a mashonisa?

Mashonisa is a common South African term for a moneylender, often one operating informally or within a community. The word alone does not establish whether a particular lender is registered or whether a specific loan agreement complies with the law. Check the lender's status and the terms before borrowing.

Understand the total

A loan with a small instalment can still cost a lot over time. Add every repayment and applicable fee to understand the total amount you will pay.

Check the agreement

Ask for the interest rate, fees, repayment dates and total cost in writing. Do not rely only on a verbal promise or a headline rate.

Protect your banking details

Never share your banking PIN or one-time passwords. If someone keeps your bank card or identity document, contact your bank or the appropriate authorities for help.

How the loan calculator works

1. Flat monthly interest illustration

This model calculates interest on the original loan amount for each month of the term.

Total interest = Loan amount × monthly rate × number of months

Total repayment = Loan amount + total interest

For example, R3,000 at 30% per month for three months gives R2,700 in illustrative interest and R5,700 in total repayment. This assumes the original amount remains the interest basis throughout the term. It is not a statement about how every moneylender calculates a loan.

2. Bank-style amortising loan illustration

This model converts the annual interest rate to a monthly rate and estimates equal repayments that pay down the balance over the selected term.

It does not include bank initiation fees, monthly service fees, credit life insurance or other charges. The actual total cost of a bank loan may therefore be higher than the calculator's estimate.

3. Why the cheapest-looking loan may not be cheapest

Compare the effective cost, all fees, repayment schedule and whether the interest rate is fixed or variable. A lower interest rate does not automatically mean a lower total cost when fees and loan terms differ.

What to do if you are struggling with loan debt

If you are already struggling to repay a loan, taking another loan to pay the first can make the problem worse. Before borrowing again, consider asking your existing lender about your options and speaking with an authorised debt counsellor if you may be over-indebted.

Steps you can take
  1. Write down every debt, the balance, interest rate, fees and next payment date.
  2. Keep copies of loan agreements, receipts and messages from lenders.
  3. Contact the lender to discuss your circumstances and ask about available arrangements.
  4. Do not promise repayments that you cannot realistically afford.
  5. Get independent help before signing a new loan or debt-consolidation agreement.

Frequently asked questions

Is every mashonisa illegal?

No. The term does not by itself determine a lender's legal status. Credit providers may be subject to registration requirements and applicable laws depending on the circumstances. Contact the NCR if you need help checking a provider or raising a concern.

Is 30% interest per month legal in South Africa?

Do not assume that a rate is legal or illegal based on the percentage alone. Applicable rules depend on the type of credit agreement and the relevant law. Ask the NCR or a qualified legal adviser to assess a specific agreement.

What is the maximum interest rate a lender can charge?

South African credit legislation and regulations set limits for different types of credit. There is no single percentage that should be applied indiscriminately to every loan. Check the current rules and the exact type of agreement.

Will a bank approve me if I have existing debt?

Approval depends on the lender's criteria, your credit profile, income, expenses and affordability assessment. No calculator can guarantee approval.

Does the calculator show the exact amount I will pay?

No. It is an illustrative comparison. Use the written repayment schedule and full cost disclosure from the actual lender to make a decision.

Can I report a lender who is threatening me?

If you are in immediate danger, contact the South African Police Service or emergency services. For a credit-related complaint, contact the NCR. Keep evidence of threats and seek appropriate help.
