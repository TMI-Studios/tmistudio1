How to price shisanyama plates

Start with the cost of the meat portion, sides, seasoning and serving materials. Add up the daily expenses that keep the business running, then compare your selling price with the total cost. Use actual portion sizes and supplier prices for a useful estimate.

Know your contribution per plate

The selling price minus the ingredients and serving cost gives the amount available to cover daily expenses. Once those expenses are covered, the remainder is estimated operating profit.

Plan for quieter days

Sales can change with the weather, location, weekends and events. Test a lower daily sales figure to see whether your business can still cover its costs.

Watch food waste

Track what you buy, what you prepare, what you sell and what remains. Waste, spoilage and oversized portions can reduce profit even when sales look strong.

Shisanyama business FAQs

How much profit can a shisanyama make per day?

Profit depends on selling prices, portion sizes, daily sales and expenses. Enter your real numbers to estimate a result for your own operation.

How do I calculate the cost of one plate?

Add the cost of all ingredients and serving materials used for one plate. Include realistic portions of meat, sides, sauces and packaging.

What is break-even sales?

Break-even is the number of plates you need to sell to cover the daily operating expenses included in the calculator. It is an estimate, not a sales guarantee.

Does the calculator include tax?

No. It estimates operating profit from the costs entered. It does not calculate income tax, VAT, loan interest or other obligations.

Can I use this for a food stall or braai business?

Yes. The tool can help with basic plate costing for a braai stall or similar food business. Change the inputs to match your menu and expenses.
