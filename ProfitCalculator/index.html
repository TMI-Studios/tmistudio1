Understand your numbers

How to price products for profit

Setting a selling price is more than adding a little extra to what you paid. A good starting point is to count your costs, decide how much profit you want to make, and check whether your price makes sense for your customers.

01. Add up your costs

Include materials or stock, packaging, delivery, payment fees and other costs that apply to each item. Don't forget to account for your own labour where appropriate.

02. Choose a selling price

Your price should cover your costs and leave room for profit. Also consider customer demand, competitor prices and the value of what you offer.

03. Check your margin

Profit margin shows what percentage of your selling price remains as profit before excluded costs. It helps you compare prices and make informed decisions.

Markup vs profit margin: what's the difference?

These two terms are related, but they are not the same. Both use profit, but they divide it by different amounts.

Measure Calculation Example
Cost All included costs added together R50
Selling price Price charged to the customer R100
Profit per item Selling price minus cost R50
Markup Profit divided by cost × 100 100%
Profit margin Profit divided by selling price × 100 50%

In this example, the R50 profit is equal to 50% of the R100 selling price, giving a 50% profit margin. But the same R50 profit is 100% of the R50 cost, giving a 100% markup.

How to calculate a selling price for a target margin

If you know your total cost per item and the profit margin you want, you can calculate a suggested price using this formula:

Selling price = Total cost ÷ (1 − target margin)

For a product that costs R50, with a target margin of 40%:

R50 ÷ (1 − 0.40) = R83.33

A selling price of approximately R83.33 gives a gross profit of R33.33 per item and a margin of about 40%, before any costs you have not included.

A target margin is a planning tool, not a guarantee of sales. Check that your price is realistic for your market and covers the expenses your business actually incurs.

Pricing tips for South African small businesses

Include the costs you might forget

Packaging, courier charges, ingredients, payment processing, damaged stock and your time can all affect profitability. Use a sensible estimate for costs that vary per item.

Separate item costs from fixed business expenses

Rent, internet, equipment and monthly subscriptions may need to be allocated across your sales. The calculator only includes the costs you enter per item, so it does not automatically account for every business overhead.

Review your prices when costs change

Supplier prices, transport and packaging can change. Recalculate your prices periodically instead of assuming last month's numbers are still accurate.

Remember tax and VAT

This calculator does not calculate income tax or VAT. If your business is VAT-registered, your pricing calculations may need to account for output VAT, deductible input VAT and whether your entered costs are VAT-inclusive or VAT-exclusive. Get appropriate tax guidance for your circumstances.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate profit per item?

Add the costs you incur for one item, then subtract that total from the selling price. If you sell an item for R100 and its included costs total R60, your profit before excluded costs is R40.

How do I calculate profit margin?

Subtract the total included cost from the selling price. Divide that profit by the selling price and multiply by 100. If the selling price is zero, the profit margin is undefined.

What is a good profit margin for a small business?

There is no single ideal margin for every business. Product type, competition, operating expenses, sales volume and customer expectations all matter. Work out your costs and research your market before setting a target.

Can I use this calculator for baking, beauty services or reselling?

Yes. Enter the relevant materials or stock costs and include packaging, transport and other per-item costs. For services, you can include an estimated labour cost in the other-cost field.

Does this calculator include VAT or income tax?

No. It calculates profit based on the amounts you enter. It does not determine your tax obligations or automatically adjust prices for VAT.

Is ProfitPal SA free?

Yes. The calculator runs in your browser and does not require an account or an external API.
