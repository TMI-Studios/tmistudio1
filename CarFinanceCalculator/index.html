How balloon payments work

A balloon payment leaves a lump sum to settle at the end of your finance term. This can make monthly instalments lower, but it does not make the outstanding amount disappear. Read WesBank's balloon payment explanation.

  • You may need to pay the balloon in cash, sell or trade in the vehicle, or apply to refinance it if the lender permits.
  • Refinancing is not guaranteed and may create a new loan with its own costs.
  • Compare the total cost of the deal, not just the monthly instalment.
  • Make sure your budget can handle running costs as well as the finance payment.
What should I include in my car budget?
  • Finance instalment and any balloon payment you need to save towards.
  • Comprehensive insurance if required by your finance agreement.
  • Fuel, servicing, repairs, tyres and licence costs.
  • Parking, tolls and other regular travel expenses.
  • Unexpected repairs and the possibility of interest-rate changes if your agreement is linked to a variable rate.

These costs vary by vehicle, distance travelled, insurer and personal circumstances. Use realistic estimates for your own situation.

Frequently asked questions

Is a balloon payment always a bad idea?

Not necessarily. It reduces the regular instalment but leaves a large amount due later. Whether it suits you depends on the contract, your plans for the vehicle and your ability to settle the final amount.

Does the calculator include every bank fee?

No. You can enter upfront fees, but this tool does not automatically know a lender's initiation fee, monthly service fee, interim interest or other charges. Check your written quote.

Does this predict what my car will be worth later?

No. It does not estimate depreciation, resale value, trade-in value or the remaining balance under every lender's contract. A vehicle's future value is uncertain.

Can I use this for a used car?

Yes, as a rough budgeting tool. Enter the price, deposit, rate, term and running costs relevant to the used vehicle. Actual finance availability and terms depend on the lender and vehicle.
