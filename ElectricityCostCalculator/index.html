How many units does R500 buy?

Electricity units are measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh). The number purchased depends on the energy rate and any charges deducted from the amount you spend.

Example: R500 at R2.85/kWh
With no fixed deductions, R500 buys approximately 175.4 kWh. If R217 in applicable charges were deducted first, R283 would remain, buying approximately 99.3 kWh at that same example rate.

These are mathematical examples, not a claim about what every household receives. Some tariffs have block rates, fixed charges, service fees, levies or other rules. Do not subtract a fixed fee unless it actually applies to your account or purchase.

Geyser electricity cost: what does it use?
A 3 kW geyser operating at full rated power for 3 hours uses 9 kWh. At R2.85/kWh, that is R25.65 per day or R769.50 over 30 days.
  1. A 3 kW geyser running at full power for 2 hours uses 6 kWh. At R2.85/kWh, that is R17.10 per day or R513 over 30 days.
  2. The difference between those two illustrative schedules is R256.50 over 30 days at that rate.
  3. A geyser does not necessarily draw full power continuously. Actual use depends on water temperature, heating cycles, insulation, thermostat settings and household hot-water demand.
  4. Before changing geyser settings or switching off power, consider household needs, manufacturer guidance and the risk of excessively hot or inadequately heated water.

How to make your electricity last longer
  1. Track your meter before and after using high-power appliances.
  2. Boil only the water you need in your kettle.
  3. Use heating and cooking appliances only as needed.
  4. Check fridge door seals and follow the manufacturer's settings.
  5. Compare your actual usage against appliance labels and meter readings.
  6. Check your current supplier's tariff and any applicable fixed charges before comparing prepaid purchases.

Savings depend on the appliance, your household habits and your tariff. No particular saving is guaranteed.

Why electricity prices differ across South Africa

Electricity pricing is not identical across South Africa. Eskom direct customers and municipal customers may have different tariffs, and customer categories can have different energy rates or fixed charges. Some tariffs also use different rates at different usage levels.

Eskom publishes annual tariff changes, while NERSA publishes decisions for electricity distributors. For the 2026/27 period, Eskom's published changes distinguish direct-customer tariffs from municipal tariff adjustments. Always check the tariff schedule that applies to your own account.

Frequently asked questions

Is R2.85/kWh the official South African rate?

No. It is an editable example rate. Your actual rate may be different depending on your supplier, municipality, tariff category and usage.

Does the calculator know my municipality's fixed fees?

No. Enter any applicable deduction yourself. The calculator does not automatically retrieve tariffs or identify your municipality.

Does it calculate tiered electricity pricing?

Not yet. It applies one entered rate to all estimated units. If your tariff uses blocks or different rates, the result may differ from your actual electricity purchase.

Will the appliance estimate match my meter exactly?

Not necessarily. It uses rated power multiplied by time. Real appliances may cycle, change power draw or use less energy than their rated maximum.
